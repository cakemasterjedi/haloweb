Emblem Display Wiring

Power and voltage sensing for the trunk-emblem screen: 12 V from an always-on fuse in the trunk fuse box (through an optional kill switch), through the INA219 and the 12 V → 5 V converter in the trunk lid, then up through the two hollow pegs in the factory peg holes: power through one, sensor wires through the other. No drilling.

Before you power it up

Power the INA219 from the board's 3.3 V, never from the converter's 5 V. Its pull-ups would put 5 V on the ESP32's data pins. And never connect 12 V to the board's BAT connector.

Notes

  • Which circuit: to keep the display running with the car off, tap an always-on (constant, terminal 30) fuse in the trunk fuse box, on the right side behind the trim cover. Find one with a test light or multimeter: lock the car, wait about 20 minutes for it to go to sleep, and check which fuses are still live. Avoid airbag, fuel pump, DSC and engine-computer fuses. The firmware's battery saver turns the display off if the battery gets low.
  • Kill switch (optional): for a car that sits for weeks, put a switch in the 12 V line right after the fuse tap so you can turn the display fully off. Use a 12 V switch rated 5 A or more: an inline rocker on a pigtail is easiest (no mounting), or a small sealed toggle tucked behind the trunk side trim near the fuse box.
  • Fuse tap: the G30 fuse box has both Mini and ATO (standard-size) blade fuses. Check the size of the fuse you tap and use the matching add-a-circuit with a 3 A fuse of the same size (a kit with both is easiest). The original fuse goes in the lower slot and the new 3 A fuse in the upper one. Fit the tap in the direction that puts its lead on the load side.
  • Chassis ground: use an existing body ground stud or bolt in the trunk lid, on bare metal. Don't ground through the emblem pegs or the painted skin.
  • Cables: each peg has a 2.7 mm wire hole. Right peg: two 28 AWG silicone wires (5 V + GND, about 1.0 mm thick each; keep the run under 1 m). Left peg: four 30 AWG silicone wires (3.3 V, GND, SDA, SCL). Use single wires, not sheathed cable, and fill each peg around the wires with silicone.
  • Shared ground: the converter's input and output grounds are common, so the INA219's GND (through the left peg to the board's I2C port) shares the same ground as the 12 V side, which it needs to read voltage.
  • Address: the INA219 is at I2C address 0x40, which doesn't clash with the board's touch, RTC, IMU or IO-expander chips.

Reading the voltage (Arduino)

#include <Wire.h>
#include <Adafruit_INA219.h>

Adafruit_INA219 ina;               // default address 0x40

void setup() {
  Wire.begin(15, 7);               // board's I2C: SDA = GPIO15, SCL = GPIO7
  ina.begin(&Wire);
}

float carVolts() {                 // voltage at VIN+ (the 12 V feed)
  return ina.getBusVoltage_V() + ina.getShuntVoltage_mV() / 1000.0;
}
